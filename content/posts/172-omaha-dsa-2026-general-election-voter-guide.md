# About

As of today, Omaha DSA has formed an Electoral Working Group, however, we are not currently running electoral campaigns, and Omaha DSA has not adopted an Electoral Strategy. We do have Members in Good Standing and allies running for office. Given that, we are not currently doing endorsements, nor do we have an endorsement process, nor are we currently supporting any races as a chapter. This does not say we discourage individuals from supporting any candidate for office; chapter members ought to support who they need to for themselves, as that helps build up skills needed for later campaigns.

We also may recommend voting for certain candidates or voting against certain candidates without making an endorsement. This should not be confused with an endorsement, and only represents the views of the people who crafted this document from the lens of creating the best possible organizing conditions for the organization. It does not represent the expenditure of the organization’s resources in support of any candidate.

Nebraska is a red state where the Republicans control a supermajority in the Legislature, the entire federal delegation to Congress, and the Governor’s office. CD-2, consisting of Douglas, Saunders, and portions of Sarpy County, is viewed as a blue dot for its votes for Democratic Presidential candidates in Nebraska’s unique split electoral voting structure, but Republican incumbent Don Bacon has held the Congressional seat since 2016.

There are now six recognized parties in the State of Nebraska:

- America First
- Democratic
- Legal Marijuana NOW
- Libertarian
- Nebraska Working People
- Republican

Anyone not registered to a party is a Nonpartisan.

This document’s goal is to be a resource to you as you fill out your ballot and consider your options. It would be a very difficult task to include every single race on the ballot in all of Omaha DSA’s jurisdiction, so we tried to include the races that were the most relevant. We hope to make it more robust in future years with every office that could appear on your ballot – any exclusions are the result of time and capacity restraints. We have included other helpful resources for researching your ballot at the end of this guide.

If you are disappointed with the options on your ballot and the positions of the candidates, know that you are not alone in that feeling. We are so excited for the future of Omaha DSA’s Electoral Working Group and getting candidates on the ballot – and soon, Socialists in Office – we can enthusiastically endorse.

As one of the newest political parties on the ballot, it seems his central function on the ballot is to draw conservative votes away from Pete Ricketts.

Pete Ricketts is worth billions thanks to his family’s fortune, and is part-owner of the Chicago Cubs. Ricketts has been a consistent enemy of the left since his initial failed run for Senate in 2006 against Ben Nelson. After that defeat, he successfully became the Governor of Nebraska in 2014 until 2023, when he was appointed to fill former Senator Ben Sasse’s seat.

During his tenure as governor, he reinstated the death penalty by funding a ballot measure to do so after a ban was successful in the legislature and consistently worked against abortion rights. When in 2016, several Republican senators in the Legislature decided to vote against his interests as governor by supporting issues like drivers’ licenses for DACA recipients and the death penalty repeal, he then devoted his personal fortune to donating to their primary opponents. As Senator, he has been a consistent ally of the Trump administration in spite of his initial opposition to his candidacy in 2016 and has [one of the most conservative voting records](https://www.govtrack.us/congress/members/pete_ricketts/456952/report-card/2024) in the entire body. We cannot emphasize enough: do not vote for Pete Ricketts.

Osborn was the president of the Bakery, Confectionery, Tobacco Workers and Grain Millers International Union Local 50G and led a strike at the Omaha Kellogg’s in 2021. After his firing at Kellogg’s, he then became a member of Steamfitters and Plumbers Local 464.

Osborn is endorsed by the Nebraska Democratic Party, the United Auto Workers (UAW), the American Federation of Government Employees (AFGE), the Communications Workers of America (CWA),  the National and Nebraska Education Association (NEA), the Nebraska State AFL-CIO, the Committee to Protect Healthcare, the National and Nebraska Sierra Club, and End Citizens’ United. Osborn is also endorsed by the J Street PAC, a PAC that supports [conditional U.S. aid to Israel](https://jstreetpac.org/jstreetpac-endorsement-criteria-and-process/).

Policy positions:

- Draining the swamp: Overturning Citizens’ United, banning Congressional stock trading, no corporate PAC money, instituting term limits, creating independent redistricting commissions, enforcing age limits, and releasing the Epstein files.
- Protecting Paychecks, Social Security, and Healthcare: Middle class tax cut, Click to Cancel, Right to Repair, limit consumer fees, targeted tariffs, break up healthcare monopolies, protect Social Security and Medicare, negotiate drug pricing, keeping the government out of “private healthcare decisions”
- Cut Waste and Balance the Budget: Establish a national audit, no more corporate handouts (including data centers giveaways.)
- Protect the Good Life: Nebraska-First Foreign Policy means, quote, “ending the war in Iran and cutting off aid to Israel until they end their aggression against Gaza and their neighbors” and “No weapon that says “Made in America” should ever be used on a school or hospital.” Also mentions standing up to health insurance monopolies, limits on data centers, having the back of First Responders (including police and firefighters),  and,  “I will work with any administration's Department of Transportation to further connect our state to the national AMTRAK system, and ensure our state’s rail network is up to date and safe.”

- Defend Our Freedoms and the Constitution: Protect the 2nd amendment, end the surveillance state, get government out of our private data, legalize medical marijuana, keep government out of medical decisions (identifies as personally pro-life but opposed to abortion bans.)

Considerations:

Osborn has been a union leader and has taken noteworthy stances on cutting off aid to Israel, protecting “private healthcare decisions” (read as things like abortion rights and trans healthcare), and limiting data centers as well as a host of other consumer protections. These are all stances that many Democrats or Independents would not be brave enough to run competitively statewide, and are in alignment with many parts of our national program and local priorities.

However, notably missing from his policy positions are issues like immigration. Osborn has previously run ads offering to help Trump “build the wall,” and played both sides of the issue. Additionally, Osborn responded to attacks from Ricketts by stating he would not support “boys in girls’ sports.” In [leaked conversations](https://www.politico.com/newsletters/playbook/2026/09/08/hitting-trumps-wall-in-nebraska-01067165), he has indicated that his stated support for a border wall (referred to as a “symbol of racism”) or stance on trans athletes were a matter of winning the election rather than personal belief. Undoubtedly “moderating” on these issues that are, unfortunately, popular statewide in Nebraska is a part of his electoral strategy and not necessarily an indication of his personal beliefs – whether or not they’d be his actions in the Senate is a continued matter of debate.

Mike Marvin [dropped out of the race](https://www.ketv.com/article/nebraska-senate-mike-marvin-drops-out-endorses-osborn/73497835) and endorsed Dan Osborn. He is the former Executive Director of the NAPE/AFSCME. His primary policy position, as you may have guessed from his party affiliation, is legal marijuana now. His website also lists ending government surveillance, repealing the Patriot Act, and ending wasteful foreign wars. He also describes, “[working with other candidates](https://marvinforsenate.com/) in the race to make sure we have a united front against Pete Ricketts in November.” His name will still appear on the ballot.

Robin Richards is a member of the Ralston School Board and a former registered Democrat who lost her primary in Legislative District 12 as a write-in candidate. There has been a lot of speculation about her newly formed party, the Working People’s Party, and its affiliation with both liberal and conservative figures in Nebraska. The Nebraska Democratic Party has [publicly accused her candidacy as tied to Crystal Rhoades](https://nebraskademocrats.org/blog/ndp-press-release-statement-on-the-nebraska-working-people-party-candidate-filing/), a current elected Democrat and frequent Democratic campaign manager, after Rhoades’ defeat in the Congressional District 2 primary. Bottom line:

Osborn is not going to be a candidate that we can count on to fight for abolishing ICE or standing up for trans rights. That being said, he would be a force for improving the conditions of workers in Nebraska and a much-needed vote in Senate against foreign wars and funding the genocide in Gaza. He is the best option of all candidates in this race and would change the makeup of our all-conservative federal delegation significantly.

## Nebraska Public Service Commission

The Nebraska Public Service Commission is a 5-member elected body that is part of the Executive Branch of the state government; however, unlike most states, Nebraska elects its PSC members per district. What the PSC does is they regulate common carriers. In common law, a common carrier is a service that is generally available to the public that transports information, people, or goods from one location to another in a transactional manner. Currently, they regulate the following:

- Telecommunications/Broadband
- Natural gas utilities
- Major oil pipelines
- Railroads
- Household goods movers
- Passenger carriers (taxis, limousines)
- Grain warehouses and dealers
- Construction of manufactured and modular homes and recreational vehicles
- High-voltage electric transmission lines
- Private water company rates
- Emergency-911

Most notably, the PSC has been responsible for blocking the Keystone XL pipeline.

The Public Service Commission has their regular meetings on the 3rd floor of The Atrium on 12th & N in Lincoln.

PSC members serve 6-year terms and are elected on the partisan ballot. They make $75,000 a year, which is set by the Legislature, but are barred from employment during office.

# Statewide Ballot Initiatives

### Constitutional Amendment 1: Legislative Term Limits

A vote “FOR” will amend the Nebraska Constitution to increase the term limit for state legislators from two consecutive four-year terms (eight years) to three consecutive four-year terms (12 years).

A vote “AGAINST” will not amend the Nebraska Constitution, so the current limit of two consecutive four-year terms will remain in effect. State legislators will continue to be ineligible for office in the Legislature for four years after serving two consecutive terms.

This constitutional amendment was brought to the ballot by a [39-10 vote of the legislature in 2025](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/bills/view_votes.php?KeyID=12916). Progressive Senators Hunt, McKinney, Spivey, and Dungan joined most of the body in voting for it – both Senator Cavanaughs and Senator Guereca voted against it. Legislative term limits were initially introduced via ballot initiative in 2000. It’s long been said that their introduction was intended to undermine Nebraska’s longest serving state senator, Ernie Chambers, and the expertise he’d built within the legislature. Term limits do not prevent former state senators from running for re-election after sitting out for a term of four years, which is what Senator Chambers ended up doing prior to choosing to retire.

Another consequence of term limits has been that it has made campaign fundraising more cutthroat – [Pete Ricketts is notorious for using his personal fortune to select his preferred elected senators](https://nebraskapublicmedia.org/en/news/news-articles/ricketts-riches-record-election-spending-shows-senators-family-far-from-done-in-nebraska/). They can also make building institutional knowledge in the body a challenge, especially for [new senators who may rely on lobbying firms and outside influence to navigate the body](https://civicnebraska.org/nebraska-term-limits-2026/) and draft legislation. This is not to say new senators are a bad thing, but it is worth noting that term limits were introduced with the intention of undermining the power of an effective and progressive political independent who often drew the ire of establishment politicians in his powerful advocacy for his community.

The campaign in support of this amendment, Tax Relief Nebraska, pitched it to voters as a property tax relief mechanism. Tax Relief Nebraska [commissioned a report that indicated this effort could raise $61 million in additional tax relief over 5 years](https://nebraskaexaminer.com/2026/04/27/report-says-legalizing-online-sports-betting-in-nebraska-could-generate-87m-over-5-years/).

Nationally, the issue of sports betting [has been studied as a potential public health risk](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12365529/) as well as [risking individual Americans’ finances](https://www.npr.org/2026/04/04/nx-s1-5773354/legal-sports-betting-research-credit-bankruptcy) in predictable ways.

Omaha, Nebraska, Public Facility Construction and Renovation Bond Measure:

A "yes" vote supports issuing $155 million in general obligation bonds to fund the construction and renovation of public facilities, including:

police buildings;

fire stations;

libraries;

park maintenance facilities;

auditorium support facilities;

arena and convention center facilities; and

public works storage facilities.

A "no" vote opposes issuing $155 million in general obligation bonds to fund the construction and renovation of public facilities, including:

police buildings;

fire stations;

libraries;

park maintenance facilities;

auditorium support facilities;

arena and convention center facilities; and

public works storage facilities.

This bond will [primarily fund a $155 million fund for the new police and fire headquarters](https://www.wowt.com/2026/08/18/omaha-city-council-vote-bond-ordinances/) [set to be built at the site of Crossroads Mall](https://nebraskapublicmedia.org/en/news/news-articles/new-181m-headquarters-for-omaha-police-fire-planned-at-crossroads/), a frequent site of protests and demonstrations.

Omaha, Nebraska, Park Improvement Projects Bond Measure

A "yes" vote supports issuing $30 million in bonds to fund improvements to parks and recreational facilities.

A "no" vote opposes issuing $30 million in bonds to fund improvements to parks and recreational facilities.

This bond would [primarily fund improvements to Tranquility Park in Northwest Omaha](https://www.wowt.com/2026/08/18/omaha-city-council-vote-bond-ordinances/). Omaha Finance Director Stephen Curtiss stated, “There was a 68 million-ish project with about 16 soccer fields that we could host, regional and potentially national tournaments. As the mayor already says, everybody was tired of all the Omaha teams going off to other cities so they could play each other. Why not have those sorts of facilities here?”
